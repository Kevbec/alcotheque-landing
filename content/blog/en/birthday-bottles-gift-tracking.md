# Birthday Bottles: How to Remember Who Gave You What

Thomas turned 40 last spring. His friends and family, knowing he loves wine and good spirits, did what people always do when they know someone is a collector. They brought bottles.

By the end of the evening, there were 23 bottles on the table. Bordeaux, Burgundy, a single malt from someone who clearly knew what they were doing, two bottles of Champagne, a rum he had never heard of, and a gin that came in a beautiful box. His wife had stacked them on the sideboard. The cards from the wrapping paper had been put somewhere. The wrapping paper itself was already in a bin bag.

Thomas is someone who takes his collection seriously. He has a cave, he tracks what he buys, he knows roughly what things are worth. But standing in front of those 23 bottles the morning after his party, coffee in hand, he realized he had absolutely no idea which bottle came from whom.

---

## The Post-It Solution Nobody Talks About

He did what most people do in this situation. He grabbed a marker and a pad of post-its.

He wrote names on small pieces of paper and stuck them to the bottles. Some he remembered clearly. The single malt was from his oldest friend Marc, who had brought it back from a trip to Islay and mentioned it twice during the evening. The Champagne on the left was from his parents. The one on the right he was less sure about.

For the bottles he genuinely could not remember, he left the post-it blank and told himself he would figure it out later.

Three weeks later, half the post-its had fallen off. The ones that remained had curled at the edges and were starting to peel the label underneath. The blank ones were still blank.

He considered writing directly on the labels with a permanent marker, the way some collectors do when they want to note a date or a source. But looking at a beautiful bottle of aged Burgundy with his handwriting across the front felt wrong. These were gifts. They deserved better.

---

## The Real Problem With Forgetting

It is easy to dismiss this as a minor inconvenience. But the context of a gifted bottle matters more than most people realize.

When Thomas eventually opened the single malt from Marc eighteen months later, on a quiet evening with his brother visiting, he knew the story behind it. He could tell his brother exactly where it came from, why Marc had chosen it, what the distillery looked like from the photos Marc had shown him. The bottle tasted the same as it would have without that context. But the experience of drinking it was entirely different.

For the bottles where the context was lost, the experience was simply drinking a bottle. Pleasant, but anonymous. No story. No connection to the person who chose it for him.

And then there was the practical dimension. When Christmas came around, Thomas wanted to give Marc something as meaningful as what Marc had given him. But he could not remember the exact bottle. He remembered it was from Islay. He thought it was Lagavulin but was not certain. He ended up buying something else and spending the whole time wondering if he had already received it.

## The Broader Pattern

Thomas is not unusual. Almost everyone who collects wine or spirits at any serious level has experienced some version of this problem.

The bottles accumulate. The occasions multiply. Birthdays, Christmas, weddings, housewarming parties, dinner guests who bring something special. Each bottle arrives with a story and a person attached to it. And without a system, those stories disappear within weeks.

Some collectors use notes apps or spreadsheets. These work until they stop working, usually when the collection grows past a certain point or when the person realizes they have been meaning to update the spreadsheet for six months and has not.

The advantage of a dedicated app is that the tracking happens at the moment of receiving, not later. Scanning a label at the end of a party takes thirty seconds. Reconstructing who gave you what six months after the fact takes much longer, and is often simply impossible.
